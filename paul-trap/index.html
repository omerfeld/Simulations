Model assumptions & equations

Mathieu and inverse-calibration equations

The one-dimensional ideal quadrupole satisfies:

d²x/dτ² + [a − 2q cos(2τ)] x = 0,   τ = Ωt/2
f_sec = β f_RF / 2,   cos(πβ) = tr(M)/2

M is the fundamental-matrix propagator over τ ∈ [0,π]. The reference measurement at a = 0 gives β₀ = 2f_meas/f_RF. We invert numerically for the first stable-band |q₀|, then calibrate the combined charge-to-mass and RF-efficiency scale.

q = (Qη_RF/m) · Fq V_RF / (d² Ω²)
a = (Qη_RF/m) · (η_DC/η_RF) Fa U_DC / (d² Ω²)

For the user's original geometry convention: 3D axial Fa = +8, Fq = −4; 3D radial and linear radial Fa = −4, Fq = +2. The signs depend on electrode-potential convention; they are not universal. For a 3D quadrupole, the other axes obey aₓ = aᵧ = −a_z/2 and qₓ = qᵧ = −q_z/2.

Limitations / model validity

This is an ideal linear Mathieu model for a single selected mode. The calibration assumes no tweezer contribution in the measured reference, no DC reference curvature, a known charge sign convention, and unchanged RF/DC efficiency ratios between states. It cannot determine physical Q/m and η independently. Geometric changes are modeled only through 1/d²; a real electrode change can modify η and multipoles.

The plotted β is restricted to the first Floquet band, 0 ≤ β ≤ 1. The monodromy discriminant detects first-band instability; near a band edge, solutions may be marginal rather than robustly trapped. Frequencies in higher Mathieu stability regions require explicit mode/branch tracking and are not represented as trustworthy low-frequency secular motion here. Pseudopotential β ≈ √(a + q²/2) is shown only for comparison.

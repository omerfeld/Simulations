    Equations, assumptions and units
    Pₐᵦₛ(1560) + Pₐᵦₛ(532) = Pgas(T,p) + Prad(T,Tambient)

    Laser absorption: Pabs = I(r⊥) · Cabs; I(r⊥) = 2P exp(−2r⊥²/w₀²)/(πw₀²). The Rayleigh quasistatic expression is Cabs = 4π(2π/λ)R³ Im[(m²−1)/(m²+2)] with m = n + i k. This is a material-property estimate, not a measured absorption cross section. Rayleigh approximation requires 2πR/λ ≪ 1; tightly focused/high-NA beams introduce additional error.

    Free-molecular gas cooling: Pgas = α A · p · c̄/(8 Tgas) · (γ+1)/(γ−1) · (T−Tgas), c̄ = sqrt[8 kB Tgas/(πmgas)], A=4πR². Not valid at small Knudsen number or for gas chemistry/thermal accommodation varying with T.

    Thermal emission: Default Rayleigh far-field approximation uses an effective spectrally constant infrared Im[(ε−1)/(ε+2)], giving Prad ∝ R³ (T⁵−Tamb⁵). Alternatively, graybody Prad=4πR² ε σ (T⁴−Tamb⁴). Neither is predictive without realistic infrared material properties; thermal loss may change during graphitization.

    Time constant: τ ≈ mpart cp/(∂Pgas/∂T+∂Prad/∂T) is a local linearized estimate, not the exact heating transient. No time-dependent particle size, chemistry, oxidation, graphitization kinetics, laser-induced desorption, photoluminescence, COM thermalization, or temperature dependence of absorption is included.

    Literature and physical interpretation

    Research on levitated nanodiamonds demonstrates that absorption-induced internal heating depends strongly on pressure, material purity and surface carbon, and is experimentally distinct from center-of-mass motional temperature. Atmospheric oxidation and low-pressure graphitization are different processes; temperature thresholds depend on the specific diamond and experimental environment.
